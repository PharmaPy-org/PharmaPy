Validating a calibration
========================

A fitted parameter vector is only as trustworthy as the checks around it.
This page walks through the shared validation workflow of
``ParameterEstimation`` on one case: fit through ``Experiment`` and
``Measurement`` objects, check that the optimizer converged, verify the
derivatives the fit used, report the parameter uncertainty and practical
identifiability under an explicit noise assumption, and capture the fit in a
``CalibrationRecord``. The same steps apply to any model callback, including
the unit operations of a ``SimulationExec`` flowsheet.

.. important::

   Every fit on this page and in the validation suite it describes is a
   **synthetic or stand-in fit**: the decay data were written for the user
   guide, the crystallizer data are generated by the model itself, and the
   Ziegler workshop data are of unrecorded origin (issue
   `#478 <https://github.com/PharmaPy-org/PharmaPy/issues/478>`__). They
   verify that the estimation contract works as documented; they are **not
   evidence of an empirical calibration** of any process.

Fitting through Experiment and Measurement
------------------------------------------

The case is the two-experiment first-order decay ``A -> B`` with an
adiabatic temperature rise of :doc:`experiment_measurement`: a ``hot`` run
that measures ``c_A`` [mol/L] at four times and ``T`` [K] once, and a
``cold`` run with one missing ``c_A`` sample. Every measurement declares the
standard deviation of its errors (``uncertainty``), so the residuals are
divided by them and are dimensionless. The fit uses PharmaPy's
Levenberg-Marquardt solver (``method='LM'``). Calibration records can
capture LM and IPOPT fits; SciPy least-squares fits (``method='scipy'`` or
``'least_squares'``) cannot be recorded yet (issue
`#534 <https://github.com/PharmaPy-org/PharmaPy/issues/534>`__).

.. testcode::

   import numpy as np
   from PharmaPy.ParamEstim import Experiment, Measurement, ParameterEstimation


   def decay(params, time_s, initial_mol_l, rise_k_l_mol=0.0, temp0_k=300.0):
       """Return c_A [mol/L] and T [K] of a first-order A -> B reaction.

       Parameters
       ----------
       params : numpy.ndarray
           Rate constant k, shape (1,) [1/s].
       time_s : numpy.ndarray
           Model grid, shape (n_times,) [s].
       initial_mol_l : float
           Initial concentration of A [mol/L].
       rise_k_l_mol : float, optional
           Adiabatic temperature rise per converted concentration [K*L/mol].
       temp0_k : float, optional
           Initial temperature [K].

       Returns
       -------
       numpy.ndarray
           Shape (n_times, 2); column 0 is c_A [mol/L], column 1 is T [K].
       """
       converted = initial_mol_l * (1 - np.exp(-params[0] * time_s))  # [mol/L]
       return np.column_stack((initial_mol_l - converted,
                               temp0_k + rise_k_l_mol * converted))


   experiments = {
       'hot': Experiment(
           {'c_A': Measurement(0, [0.0, 1.0, 2.0, 4.0],  # [s]
                               [2.010, 1.200, 0.750, 0.265],  # [mol/L]
                               uncertainty=0.02, units='mol/L'),
            'T': Measurement(1, 2.0, 325.6, uncertainty=0.5, units='K')},
           args=(2.0,), kwargs={'rise_k_l_mol': 20.0}),
       'cold': Experiment(
           {'c_A': Measurement(0, [0.5, 1.5, 3.0],  # [s]
                               [0.785, np.nan, 0.220],  # [mol/L]
                               uncertainty=[0.01, 0.01, 0.02], units='mol/L')},
           args=(1.0,), kwargs={'rise_k_l_mol': 10.0, 'temp0_k': 290.0})}

   estimator = ParameterEstimation(decay, [0.3], experiments,
                                   name_params=['k'])  # seed k [1/s]
   params, covariance, info = estimator.estimate_parameters(method='LM',
                                                            verbose=False)
   print(f"k = {params[0]:.4f} 1/s")
   layout = estimator.get_residual_layout()
   print(len(layout), int(layout['observed'].sum()))

.. testoutput::

   k = 0.5011 1/s
   14 7

``get_residual_layout()`` names every entry of the weighted residual vector
(experiment, measurement, model-output field, time and whether it is
observed): 14 entries, of which 7 are observed. Unobserved entries (the
missing sample, the times at which ``T`` is not measured) are exactly zero,
so the experiments may be given in any order and each measurement keeps its
own grid. ``tests/test_calibration_validation_decay.py`` checks that
reordering the experiments or the measurements changes neither the fit nor
the residual of any named entry.

Checking convergence
--------------------

``info['success']`` and ``info['message']`` state how the optimizer
stopped; ``info['tolerances']`` holds the stopping criteria in force, the
given options merged over the optimizer's defaults (see
:doc:`../advanced_usage`). A fit that stops without converging, for example
at the evaluation limit ``max_fun_eval``, warns with a ``RuntimeWarning``
and its identifiability summary starts with ``FIT DID NOT CONVERGE``.

.. testcode::

   print(info['success'], info['message'])
   print(info['tolerances'])

.. testoutput::

   True Small step
   {'max_fun_eval': 100, 'eps_1': 1e-08, 'eps_2': 1e-08, 'tol_fun': 1e-12}

Verifying derivatives
---------------------

The optimizer, the covariance and the identifiability report all use the
weighted residual Jacobian. Here it is the estimator's default forward
difference, with a step of ``1e-3 * |k|``. ``check_derivatives`` compares it
with an independent central difference of the weighted residuals at the
accepted parameters:

.. testcode::

   check = estimator.check_derivatives()
   print(check.jacobian_source, check.formulas)
   print(f"relative error {check.relative_error[0]:.1e}")

.. testoutput::

   finite_difference ('central',)
   relative error 5.8e-04

The relative error is of the order of the forward step relative to ``k``
(``1e-3``): it is the forward difference's own truncation error, which this
closed-form case lets the test suite derive exactly. A wrong sensitivity
would differ at order one. For a model solved by an ODE integrator with
relative tolerance ``solver_rtol``, pass ``rel_step`` of about
``solver_rtol**(1/3)``. The reference is then accurate to about
``C * rel_step**2 + solver_rtol / rel_step`` for well-scaled parameters
(see "Verifying derivatives" in :doc:`../advanced_usage`), where the
truncation constant ``C`` depends on the model: for an order parameter
such as the growth order ``g`` of a crystallization rate ``k_g *
sigma**g``, each derivative multiplies by ``ln(sigma)``, so ``C`` scales
with ``ln(sigma)**2`` (about 15 at the supersaturation of the crystallizer
stand-in below), and correct sensitivities can exceed the bound with
``C = 1`` several times over. The validation suite therefore judges
solver-backed checks against that error model times a documented safety
factor; ``derivative_bound`` in
``tests/test_calibration_validation_crystallizer.py`` is the worked
example. ``bounds`` keeps every evaluation inside the parameters' valid
domain.

Uncertainty and identifiability
-------------------------------

``get_identifiability`` builds a report from the accepted fit only. Its
``noise_assumption`` states the error model the uncertainty rests on; here
the declared standard deviations are treated as relative weights whose
common scale is re-estimated from the residual mean square
(``include_mse=True``, the default; pass ``include_mse=False`` to treat them
as absolute).

.. testcode::

   report = estimator.get_identifiability()
   print(report.noise_model, report.variance_scale_estimated)
   print(report.noise_assumption.split('; ')[0])
   print(f"s2 = {report.residual_mean_square:.3f} [-], "
         f"SE(k) = {report.standard_errors[0]:.5f} 1/s")
   lower, upper = report.confidence_intervals[0]  # [1/s]
   print(f"95% interval [{lower:.4f}, {upper:.4f}] 1/s")
   print(report.identifiable, report.unresolved_directions)

.. testoutput::

   declared_uncertainty True
   Measurement errors are assumed independent, zero-mean and Gaussian, with the standard deviations declared by the measurements (Measurement.uncertainty)
   s2 = 0.341 [-], SE(k) = 0.00435 1/s
   95% interval [0.4905, 0.5118] 1/s
   True ()

The data resolve ``k``: the report is ``identifiable`` when the covariance
is defined and no direction of the Jacobi-scaled Jacobian has a singular
value at or below the ``resolution_floor``, the error bound of the
sensitivities (here the forward-difference truncation and rounding).
Resolved directions do not guarantee that the uncertainty is available:
an exact fit, for example, has a zero residual mean square, so with
``include_mse=True`` its covariance, standard errors and intervals are NaN
while ``identifiable`` is True. Read ``report.covariance_diagnostic`` (None
when the covariance is available) before reading intervals and standard
errors. ``report.summary()`` prints the whole account,
``report.to_frame()`` the per-parameter table.

For sensitivities integrated by an ODE solver, the default
``jacobian_rtol`` (machine precision) does not hold, and the solver
``rtol`` understates the error too: CVODES controls the sensitivity error
relative to the sensitivities, but only to a modest multiple of ``rtol``.
Pass a bound established by ``check_derivatives`` as ``jacobian_rtol``, as
the solver-backed modules below do.

The report is also the hand-off to model-based experiment design (issue
`#355 <https://github.com/PharmaPy-org/PharmaPy/issues/355>`__), which will
consume ``information_matrix`` (``J @ J.T`` of the weighted Jacobian),
``fisher_information`` (the same divided by the estimated variance scale
when ``include_mse=True``) and ``unresolved_directions`` (the parameter
combinations the current data do not resolve):

.. testcode::

   print(report.parameter_names, report.information_matrix.shape)
   ratio = report.information_matrix[0, 0] / report.fisher_information[0, 0]
   print(f"information / Fisher information = {ratio:.3f} = s2")

.. testoutput::

   ('k',) (1, 1)
   information / Fisher information = 0.341 = s2

Capturing a CalibrationRecord
-----------------------------

Finally, record the fit with its data, provenance and optimizer settings
(see :doc:`calibration_records`), and check that the record reruns:

.. testcode::

   from PharmaPy.Records import CalibrationRecord, DataSource, ModelRevision

   record = CalibrationRecord.from_estimation(
       estimator, experiments, case='decay-validation',
       parameter_units=['1/s'],
       model=ModelRevision('decay', revision='calibration_validation.rst, v1'),
       sources=[DataSource(
           'guide', 'synthetic',
           'synthetic values written for the user guide; no external data')])
   loaded = CalibrationRecord.from_json(record.to_json())
   replay_rtol = 4 * np.finfo(float).eps  # [-], four units in the last place
   rerun = loaded.rerun(decay, rtol=replay_rtol, atol=0.0)  # atol [1/s]
   print(loaded == record, loaded.solver.method,
         loaded.solver.outcome['stop_criterion'])
   print(f"k = {rerun.params_convg[0]:.4f} 1/s")

.. testoutput::

   True LM Small step
   k = 0.5011 1/s

Stand-in checks of solver-backed cases
--------------------------------------

The validation suite runs the same workflow on three stand-in cases, one
module each:

* ``tests/test_calibration_validation_decay.py`` (core test lane): the
  decay case above, including the derivative check of exact sensitivities,
  of a fit with a fixed parameter and near a lower bound, experiment and
  measurement reordering, bitwise repeatability, unchanged observations
  after the analyses and a bootstrap, both covariance forms of the report,
  a truncated fit and the ``estimate_parameters`` to record to rerun path.
* ``tests/test_calibration_validation_ziegler.py`` (Assimulo lane): the
  workshop's multi-temperature Ziegler reactor fit of
  ``tests/test_records_ziegler_case.py``, whose CVODES sensitivities are
  checked against central differences of complete solves and whose
  identifiability report uses a ``jacobian_rtol`` established by that
  check. Its noise assumption differs from the decay case: the workshop
  data declare no measurement uncertainties, so the fit is unweighted
  (``noise_model`` ``'identity'``); both measured fields are molar
  concentrations [mol/L], and one common error variance is re-estimated
  from the residuals. Repeatability and record capture, reload and rerun
  of this fit are checked by ``tests/test_records_ziegler_case.py``.
* ``tests/test_calibration_validation_crystallizer.py`` (Assimulo lane): a
  seeded, growth-only ``BatchCryst`` (method of moments) fitted to data the
  model generates at known growth parameters, with samples after the charge
  (issue `#328 <https://github.com/PharmaPy-org/PharmaPy/issues/328>`__),
  noise-free and with seeded Gaussian noise whose standard deviations the
  measurements declare. It checks parameter recovery, the CVODES
  sensitivities at the default and at tightened solver tolerances, the
  strong correlation of the growth rate constant and order that the report
  exposes, unchanged observations, convergence, and record capture and a
  bitwise rerun. The fitted
  subset is selected through ``mask_params`` (an unmasked sensitivity solve
  stalls, issue `#222 <https://github.com/PharmaPy-org/PharmaPy/issues/222>`__),
  the record names all 13 kinetic parameters (issue
  `#535 <https://github.com/PharmaPy-org/PharmaPy/issues/535>`__), and the
  sample times have no replicates (issue
  `#314 <https://github.com/PharmaPy-org/PharmaPy/issues/314>`__).

Experiment and measurement reordering and the residual-layout alignment
are checked on the decay case only. Run the Assimulo modules with ``pixi
run -e assimulo test-assimulo`` (see ``TESTING.md``).

Adopting the December demonstrators
-----------------------------------

The crystallization (issue
`#358 <https://github.com/PharmaPy-org/PharmaPy/issues/358>`__) and
bioreactor (issue
`#361 <https://github.com/PharmaPy-org/PharmaPy/issues/361>`__) calibration
cases are not yet on the main branch; the stand-ins above take their place.
When each lands, it must:

* run this workflow on the real case and its data;
* check convergence and the recorded stopping tolerances
  (``info['success']``, ``info['message']``, ``info['tolerances']``);
* check experiment identity: reordering the experiments and the
  measurements leaves the fit and every named row of
  ``get_residual_layout`` unchanged, the residuals and accepted outputs are
  aligned with the layout, repeated fits are identical, and the
  observations are unchanged after the uncertainty, identifiability,
  derivative and bootstrap analyses;
* check derivatives with steps matched to the solver tolerances
  (``rel_step`` of about ``solver_rtol**(1/3)``, or tighter solver
  tolerances for a sharper check), passing ``bounds`` for parameters with
  a restricted valid domain;
* report identifiability with declared measurement uncertainties, so that
  the noise assumption is stated rather than defaulted as in the
  unweighted Ziegler stand-in, and with a ``jacobian_rtol`` established by
  the derivative check;
* record the fit with ``CalibrationRecord``;
* link the open defects that affect its public estimation path;
* add the case to this validation suite as its own module.
